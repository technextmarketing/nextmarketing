🏢 Client Overview

ShieldNet Security Solutions is a cybersecurity services firm based in Kuala Lumpur, serving mid-market and enterprise clients across banking, healthcare, and government sectors. With a team of 8 business development reps and an average deal size of RM85,000, ShieldNet competes against both local system integrators and large MNC vendors such as Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, and Fortinet.

⚠️ The Challenge

  • Win rate stuck at 31% for 3 consecutive quarters despite growing pipeline volume
  • New reps took 16 weeks to reach full productivity — losing deals during the critical first months
  • No standardised objection handling: each rep improvised responses to competitor comparisons differently
  • Sales manager spent ~6 hours/week coaching individual reps on the same questions repeatedly
  • 5 active competitor scenarios (Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Fortinet, local SIs, in-house IT) had no documented counter-messaging
  • Proposals were formatted inconsistently, with varying quality across the team — affecting buyer trust
  • No library of proof points or client success stories for reps to reference during enterprise negotiations

🗺️ Our Approach

Week 1–2
Discovery & Win/Loss Analysis

Interviewed 6 reps and reviewed 40 closed deals (24 won, 16 lost). Mapped 5 recurring competitor scenarios and identified the top 12 buyer objections. Documented current messaging gaps and ranked content needs by deal-stage impact.

Week 3–4
Battle Card Development (Phase 1)

Built 5 competitor battle cards — one per major rival. Each card: positioning summary, key differentiators, head-to-head comparison table, 3 disqualifying questions, and a proof point. Shared draft with sales manager for real-world accuracy check.

Week 5–6
Objection Handling Guide

Compiled the 12 most common objections into a structured "Objection → Reframe → Proof" format. Each entry included 2 alternative responses (executive vs. technical buyer), a mini case reference, and a recommended follow-up asset. Formatted as a quick-reference PDF and a searchable Google Doc.

Week 7–8
Mini Case Studies & Proof Library

Wrote 4 one-page case studies (anonymised) across banking, healthcare, SME, and government segments. Created a proof point library with 22 client statistics, regulatory compliance references, and third-party validation points reps could pull into any conversation.

Week 9–10
Onboarding Playbook & Training Deck

Consolidated all assets into a 38-page New Rep Playbook — ICP profiles, discovery questions, qualification framework, proposal checklist, and content map by deal stage. Built a 20-slide training deck used in the first enablement session.

Week 11–13
Live Rollout & Coaching Calibration

Ran 2 × 90-minute enablement workshops with the full sales team. Tracked first usage across 14 active deals. Gathered rep feedback, updated 3 battle cards with field corrections, and handed over a maintenance guide so the team could keep content fresh independently.

🃏 Battle Cards Delivered

Enterprise Vendor

ShieldNet vs. Palo Alto

Focuses on total cost of ownership, local support SLA, and implementation speed. Key disqualifier: "Ask about their onboarding timeline for a 200-seat deployment."

US Cloud Vendor

ShieldNet vs. CrowdStrike

Positions ShieldNet's on-premise hybrid option and data residency compliance for banking and government clients under Bank Negara and PDPA frameworks.

Network Security

ShieldNet vs. Fortinet

Highlights managed service model vs. self-managed appliances. Addresses total headcount cost and in-house skill gap — ideal for SME and mid-market buyers.

Local Competitors

ShieldNet vs. Local SIs

Differentiates on SOC-as-a-Service maturity, 24/7 response SLA, and certified analyst team. Uses case studies from comparable-sized clients as proof points.

Internal Competition

ShieldNet vs. In-House IT Team

Addresses the "we'll just build it internally" objection. ROI calculator showing 3-year cost comparison: internal team (RM480k/year fully loaded) vs. ShieldNet managed service (RM156k/year). Backed by compliance gap analysis specific to their sector.
